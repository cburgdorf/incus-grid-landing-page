— Not a forecast

The forecast tells you about tomorrow. IncusGrid tells you about the season.

Which passage do crews take in June? Where do boats spend the winter? Which month brings the Meltemi, and which wintering harbour sees fewer thunderstorms in November? Answers that only show up when you look at many years at once.

01

Sailing routes

Plan crossings on the experience of thousands of crews.

Thousands of real passages show when a crossing makes sense, in which direction, and where crews set off and arrive. The prevailing winds, good places to wait for a weather window and a safe landfall after a night at sea are already built in. From Sicily to the Ionian Islands, for example, most boats head east in May and June and sail back in September.

  • Popularity per month and direction
  • The most popular routes in your current map view
  • Share a route with your crew

02

Wind along the route

Know the wind before you cast off.

Every route shows the wind you can expect on it, month by month: where it comes from, how strong it blows, how much of the passage you sail upwind, on a beam reach or downwind, and how often the sea stays calm. From Sicily to the Ionian Islands in May: wind from the northwest, Bft 3, more than half of the way downwind.

  • Prevailing wind and Beaufort force for every month
  • Upwind, beam reach or downwind on the route’s course
  • Sea state along the route

03

Boat stays

Where other boats stay.

Where do crews leave their boat for two months in summer, and where do they spend the winter? Boat stays show the harbours and anchorages where boats paused — from a few days to more than six months, by season.

  • Filter by length of stay
  • See in which months boats stay at a place
  • Spot popular wintering harbours

04

Hotspots

Find the quieter anchorage.

Where do boats crowd in August, and where is there still room? Hotspots shows how busy each sailing area is, month by month. The Ionian and the Saronic Gulf glow, while Andros, a day’s sail from Athens, stays calm.

  • From quiet to crowded
  • Every month of the year
  • Busiest and quietest months at any point

05

Spot report

Everything about one place, in one card.

Press and hold any place on the map, or right-click it, and the spot report brings it all together for the month you choose: wind and sea state, thunderstorms, how well the terrain shields it, and the boat stays and routes nearby. Gaios on Paxos in September: calm or light wind most of the time, a calm or slight sea, and thunderstorms on about one day in six. The month strip shows November as the month with the most thunderstorms.

  • Wind, sea state and thunderstorms for one place and month
  • Boat stays and routes within 5 nautical miles
  • Every month rated at a glance, as a link for your crew

06

Wind

How much northerly wind should you expect?

Pick a direction and a month: in July the central Aegean turns deep blue. That’s the Meltemi. Tap a spot near Mykonos and you see it blow from the north in 60% of all July hours or more.

  • Strength and direction for every month
  • Wind by time of day
  • Tap any point for its numbers

07

Waves

Know the sea state before you go.

How many days per month bring waves of 2.5 m or more? In January the reach of the Mistral across the Gulf of Lion stands out clearly.

  • From calm to very rough seas
  • Swell and short, steep seas
  • Every month of the year

08

Thunderstorm Risk

Which harbour sees fewer thunderstorms?

Thunderstorm Risk brings together how often thunderstorms occur, how violent they are and how mountains, headlands and open sea shape them, for every month and down to single bays. Tap one place, then another, and see how much more or less risk the second one has. Preveza and Messolonghi are both popular for wintering: in November, Messolonghi has 27% less thunderstorm risk.

  • Thunderstorm days, lightning density and terrain in one value
  • Differences down to single bays
  • Tap two places to compare them

09

Lightning data

How often, how violent, how likely.

Behind the risk are three layers you can open on their own, on the same scale in every month. In September the Mediterranean shows why you need all three: thunderstorms are most frequent along the Adriatic and Ionian coasts, the lightning is densest over the sea between Italy and Greece, and conditions that favour storms reach far into the south.

  • Thunderstorm Days: how often storms occur, 2013–2025
  • Lightning Density: how violent they are
  • Thunderstorm Potential: where they can build, 1994–2023

10

Terrain Exposure

Same bay, different shielding.

Thunderstorm Risk tells you which bay. Terrain Exposure tells you which side of it: surrounding hills can shield an anchorage from lightning, and the layer shows how well the terrain protects each part of a bay, so you can pick the better spot before you drop anchor.

  • Compare spots within one bay
  • Detailed 30 m elevation data

11

Seabed

Sand or seagrass?

Before you drop the anchor: the Seabed layer shows sand, mud, rock, seagrass and coral along the coast, from the Mediterranean to the tropics. In the Bay of Palma a wide band of seagrass runs along the shore, and the sand lies further out. Anchoring on Posidonia is banned in more and more places, so it pays to know before you arrive.

  • Sand, mud, rock, seagrass and coral
  • Europe and shallow tropical coasts
  • Open to everyone

Our story

Born from a close call.

In October 2024, our family was sailing the Ionian Sea when lightning struck right next to our boat. That moment raised a simple question: are we safer here, or on the other side of the bay? IncusGrid started as the answer — and grew into a map of everything we wished we had known before that season.

Caution

IncusGrid shows historical patterns for planning. It is not a weather forecast or a navigation aid. Routes are drawn schematically, and boat stays are derived from pauses in boat reports, not confirmed berths. Always check current forecasts, official charts and local conditions before you go to sea.
